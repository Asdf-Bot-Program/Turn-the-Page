The message from Olred arrived before the second competition: A Khajiit scholar named Aldric Voss had died recently, without heirs, and his entire collection was going to auction. This would have been a footnote in a busy period except for one item in the catalogue, a manuscript titled Private Research: Institutional History of the Wandering Compass: Incomplete, which contained one of the only known written references to a name the group had found carved above the sea at Ysmgar's Beach, Solveig. Qrowe, Mevanos, and Volg split off to follow that thread south to Elsweyr while the rest headed for Old Orsinium and the second competition. Rimmen received them with the particular energy of a city that takes commerce seriously and treats most other concerns as secondary to it. The group discussed their approach in the way people discuss approaches when at least two of them have enough money to simply buy their way through most obstacles. This conversation was interrupted by a small Alfiq who had apparently been listening and found the preamble unnecessary. She identified herself as Daro'vassi, confirmed the auction, gave them the location and the time, and left to collect the other attendees before anyone had fully processed her arrival. The auction would begin in minutes outside the Hall of the Lunar Champion. They went to the bar to wait, which is where they found Ahriman. Mevanos and Ahriman had the kind of history that is described as somewhat agreeable, which in Telvanni terms means they had each decided the other was not worth active hostility. Ahriman was here for Aldric; he had known him, professionally, and wanted to preserve what could be preserved of his unfinished work. He explained this without sentimentality and with the particular territorial quality of someone establishing that they have a legitimate claim before anyone suggests otherwise. They agreed, cautiously, not to get in each other's way. Volg, who had no interest in the academic dimensions of this arrangement, went further into the bar and listened to the people who actually knew things about Aldric Voss in the way that locals know things about someone who lived nearby and rarely came out. What the employees said was that Aldric was strange, solitary, and possibly connected to something underground, and that there were rumors about his death involving foul play. He kept this to himself until he could give it to someone who would know what to do with it. He also acquired a copy of the auction's item list by the method of taking it from someone who had one, which he passed to Mevanos on the way out. Ahriman, having assessed the situation, decided to join them. Outside the Hall of the Lunar Champion, the assembled attendees were not what the group expected. Political representatives from the Ebonheart Pact, Vvardenfell and Eastmarch specifically, stood in clusters with the bearing of people who were here for reasons that had nothing to do with Aldric Voss's scholarly legacy, with very few actual researchers present. Mevanos recognized a face from the Topee-Rajta Baths; the Dunmer man, Drevna, was standing apart from the others in quiet conversation with an Altmer woman he did not recognize. Both of them were avoiding the Sugar Falls group with the specific quality of people who are actively choosing not to make eye contact rather than simply not noticing someone. Daro'vassi explained the rules with the efficiency of someone who had done this many times and had no patience for questions she had not invited: thirty minutes to examine the collection, then the auction would begin. Items could be purchased outright before the auction for a premium. Magical wards prevented intentional harm, physical or magical, within the grounds. This last detail prompted Volg to hand Mevanos a quantity of weapons to be stored in a magical pack; the amount of such was somewhere between impressive and absurd. Mevanos accepted with the expression of a man who had stopped being surprised by Volg but had not stopped being inconvenienced by him as they moved toward the estate.
The building was everything a lifetime of serious scholarship looked like when it had been organized by someone who cared more about access than aesthetics: walls of shelves, books categorized with the thoroughness of someone who had been at this for decades, the specific smell of paper and accumulated time with known hot ticket items sat near the center of the room; oddly, the manuscript was not there. Mevanos and Ahriman found Daro'vassi and asked directly for its location; she knew exactly where it was almost immediately. She also knew it was not available for a simple pre-auction purchase; someone had already expressed interest. She pointed across the room to where Drevna and the Altmer woman were looking at the manuscript. The approach was made when the Altmer woman introduced herself as Erevyn, Drevna's business partner at The Wandering Compass. Mevanos and Ahriman explained that they also needed the manuscript that they had an interest in. However, Drevna was firm in that The Wandering Compass also needed this manuscript and that this was non-negotiable. A threat was made in the interval by Mevanos toward Drevna's life, which caused Erevyn to step smoothly in front of the conversation the way a person steps in front of something that is about to fall over. She asked them to explain the Ivory Wolf situation in more detail. Once it was fully explained to them, Drevna and Erevyn exchanged a look that carried the weight of people who had just received information that changed the shape of something they already knew was important. Drevna excused himself to communicate with someone while Erevyn kept them talking about the situation at hand. Once Drevna returned, he said the Wandering Compass was withdrawing their offer on the manuscript. In exchange, the group would meet them in Morthal after the auction was over; the higher-ups needed to speak with them. He said this was the first time something like this had happened and that it was something they were supposed to have known about and hadn't. The urgency in that last part was genuine; whatever they had just triggered by describing the Ivory Wolf situation, it had reached something that mattered. As Mevanos agreed, Drevna and Erevyn rescinded their offer and left the auction before it began. Ahriman bought the manuscript outright, and Mevanos and Qrowe acquired other volumes for their collections with the focused efficiency of people who know what they are looking for in a room full of things worth looking at. Volg bought several books of questionable content with the energy of someone who had wanted to keep this purchase a secret from just about everyone. After the auction, Ahriman found Daro'vassi in the way of someone who had been waiting for the crowd to thin before saying what he needed to say. They talked about Aldric with the specific quiet of people who are mourning someone without performing it. She had something for him: a pair of reading glasses and a personal journal, set aside specifically for Ahriman since Ahriman was the closest thing he had to a friend and had wanted him to have them. Ahriman received these with the composure of a man who had decided some time ago not to show things in public and was executing that decision imperfectly in this moment. He thanked her and did not say more than that as the group left with their books and a meeting in Morthal waiting for them. Old Orsinium greeted the competition group consisting of Chronus, Mia, Wylarin, Liliana, and Bayard with the weight of something very old that had survived its own destruction and was not yet done. Tents from various Orc clans dotted the approach, clans that had come to watch, to witness, to form opinions. A Fighters Guild proctor, a Nord who had wanted to see Orcish cultural events in action and had been given paperwork instead, read from his sheets with the slightly deflated energy of someone whose assignment had not delivered on its premise. The challenge was called The Endurance of Time. A pilgrimage through Old Orsinium to Malacath's sacred forge at the end and back again, representing the forging of Malacath from the ashes of Trinimac. The Grey Snow had gone first, benefiting from winning the initial competition, and had already returned; with that, the group's time started now. Inside the ruins, the presence of Grey Snow mercenaries was immediately apparent to anyone who knew what to look for, and Wylarin knew exactly what to look for, yet they continued. Chronus expressed his opinions about the situation as they moved: that Clan Agalor was putting itself and everyone around it in unnecessary danger, that this entire endeavor was being conducted foolishly. Wylarin did not engage with this while Mia simply explained that they were just trying to survive. The conversation stopped there, but the tension from it did not. As they continued, they noticed that behind them came another group. At the front of that group walked someone they recognized, which created the specific adjustment required when a person you have categorized one way turns out to belong to an entirely different category. Gulraam, who had been, until this moment, primarily known to the group as a cheerful and relentless consumer of alcohol who followed them around without fully understanding why, was walking at the head of the group. He was dressed differently; he was carrying himself differently; it became clear with increasing certainty as he approached, not just a member of this group; he was its leader. The group in question turned out to be one of the most powerful clans in Wrothgar, Clan Igrun. Kushrakh was with that group as Clan Igrun's feelings about Clan Agalor were visible in how they moved, the wariness of a clan that had been asked to validate something they had not yet decided deserved validation. They were not hostile; they were simply watching as Gulraam made clear that Clan Igrun would not fight Clan Agalor's battles for them. The group was here to observe and support, not to substitute. As the pilgrimage moved forward, the Grey Snow contingent inside the ruins was not there by coincidence; they had been placed there by Yegoth during their run. The group went against them in a way that they went against all obstacles, through a combination of capability that turns an ambush into a problem the ambusher should have thought harder about. The leader of the Grey Snow contingent made the specific error of drawing Mia's full attention, the result of which was decisive in a way that left a useful piece of evidence available for later consultation. They then made it to the forge and made their way back outside. When the group returned, the proctor informed them they had lost; the Grey Snow had been faster. What followed was the specific process of changing a wrong answer through available evidence. Wylarin described what she had recognized inside: Grey Snow members, placed deliberately, present in numbers that constituted interference rather than coincidence. Kushrakh confirmed it from his knowledge of the Grey Snow's operational patterns while Mia animated the relevant portion of the contingent's former leader and let him speak to the matter directly. The biggest thing was that Gulraam vouched for the group, which went a long way in convincing the others who watched this. The proctor, a Nord who had come here to witness Orcish cultural tradition in action and had instead witnessed something considerably more complicated, ruled in Clan Agalor's favor by default; the second competition belonged to them. Mia stayed behind to talk to Gulraam and Clan Igrun about what a formal arrangement between the clans might look like while the rest returned to Clan Agalor to bring Morning the news. By the time Shel and Morning joined the conversation in the conference space, Gulraam had already moved past the formalities to the substance of what he thought and offered them delicious food that had been prepared especially for them. He was direct about the underhanded quality of what the Grey Snow had done, simply making it clear that it was despicable. Shel found the opening and took it as she talked about Clan Shatul, about the exile, about making a choice that was probably foolish in practical terms, but because of her situation the alternative was to keep pretending to be something she had never been. She said this without asking for sympathy; she was simply explaining how things had been. Gulraam said he had his own beliefs and imposed none of them on his allies. He said this with the directness of someone who means exactly what they say, but he also said that Clan Agalor needed to be able to fight for itself and offered to send people from Clan Igrun to help with that. Not to fight their battles, but to give them the capability to fight their own, to train them despite the pacifist lifestyle that they have had up to this point. Shel saw the wisdom of his words and accepted. The arrangement was finalized by the time they separated, and Clan Igrun and Clan Agalor went their respective ways with something neither of them had at the start of the day.
