The third competition began before dawn with Morning laying out the shape of it at Pariah's Pinnacle, the entirety of Wrothgar as the hunting ground, from sun up to sun down, the final count judged outside Old Orsinium at the end of the day. Three groups, three portions of the map, every animal and beast that counted toward the tally moving through terrain that both sides had been operating in for months. The Grey Snow had the same idea about splitting up; however, that in itself would soon prove to be a problem. Group one consisted of Mia, Volg, Wylarin, and Evening; they took the upper portion of Wrothgar. Group two consisted of Gulraam, Bjorn, and Tyra with their Clan Agalor companions, who took the Sorrow Mountain Range through the middle. And finally, there was group three, consisting of Chronus, Ahriman, Liliana, and Morning, who took the bottom part of Wrothgar. Each group had its route, and each group had its starting point as the day began. The Grey Snow's interference was consistent and deliberate without being direct; they were still in a formal competition, and open aggression would end them the way it had ended them in Old Orsinium. However, using a loophole, they found that what they could do was drive away quarry. Disturb areas before the Clan Agalor groups reached them, create noise at the right distances and in the right directions to push animals toward their own hunters and away from everyone else's. They were effective, they were infuriating, and they knew exactly how to manipulate the land to their favor. Evening, Volg, Mia, and Wylarin adapted, however, with Horkers, Chub Loons, and Bears. The upper portion of Wrothgar provided what the Grey Snow could not fully clear before the group reached it, and when they found the Giants, the Grey Snow's disruption strategy stopped mattering for a while because some problems require your full attention regardless of what else is happening nearby. In the Sorrow Mountain Range, Gulraam's presence did what Gulraam's presence tends to do when he is sober: it organized the energy around him into something purposeful. The Clan Agalor members who had come with his group were emboldened by it. On top of that, Bjorn and Tyra knew the terrain and used it, leading to the middle portion of Wrothgar being produced well. In the bottom portion, Liliana and Ahriman found a solution to the Grey Snow interference that was more direct than avoiding it. Liliana walked up to the Grey Snow members causing the disruptions, who seemed to be younger Orcs, as Ahriman cast a visual on the Khajiit. The result was a massive werewolf standing where a Khajiit had been, which produced the specific response that massive unexpected werewolves tend to produce in people who had been quietly disrupting a hunting competition and were not prepared for escalation of this kind. The Grey Snow members left with their tails tucked and a warm spot in their pants; they did not return. While this was happening, Morning and Chronus were somewhere else on the route, having the conversation that had been waiting since the Bloody Knoll. She tried to understand where he was coming from, the argument for letting the dream go, for finding somewhere else, for stopping before the losing became total. She could follow his logic; she could not follow it to its conclusion, as they did not resolve the argument. They agreed that they disagreed and that Chronus was still here and still willing to help, which was the honest version of where they were with each other. The groups converged as the light shifted toward late afternoon, making their way toward the judges' station outside Old Orsinium with what they had accumulated across the day. The day had been difficult, and the interference had been real, and the count was still good. Then the group noticed something was standing in the road. Gulraam, Bjorn, and Tyra did not know the figure, as they moved to the side and stayed there in the specific way of people who understand that some situations require watching rather than joining. The Ivory Wolf stood in the center of the road with a circle of freezing fog rising around the group, sealing them inside. He raised the greatsword as he said he was sorry, but he said he could not let them pass. He did not attack immediately; he looked conflicted in a way that had never been fully visible in their previous encounters. He was distracted, fighting something internal, present in a way the compulsion usually suppressed, especially so after Wylarin said that they knew of his past and that Solveig had been looking for him. Regardless of all of this, he still attacked, but the attacks the group landed were landing harder than they should have been because the thing they were landing them on was not fully committed to the fight. Mia's blood blade lodged in his shoulder; Volg's rope caught his legs; Wylarin's shadow clone found the angle and connected. He took all of it and kept moving, and then he stopped in the middle of a swing, and the greatsword went down into the ground as the fog wall began to dissolve. Standing on the other side just in front of him stood Solveig. The moment he saw her, everything else in the sequence became secondary. Thousands of years of carrying her name to cliffs above the sea, and this was the other side of that: her face, in the actual world, looking at him. The binding did not have a parameter for this, as Delvrath had not built one. The resistance Styrr had been developing for millennia was waiting for exactly the right push, and this was it. He spoke quickly because he knew how long he had. He was being controlled by Delvrath, a Daedric entity, the spirit from Granny Lexy's story made real and currently losing its hold on the person standing in front of them. Delvrath had been summoned by Yegoth, and by the Argonian they knew as Vos-Greel. But Vos-Greel was not in control of himself; his body was being operated by an ancient Corelanya necromancer named Calintar, who had been using Vos's form through a Ghostweld Crystal. Calintar's goal was to continue summoning Delvrath and, through Delvrath the Ivory Wolf, build towards a complete takeover of Solstice. All of it- the Grey Snow, the supply disruptions, the competitions- was infrastructure for something larger than Yegoth's personal vendetta. As he finished saying this, the sky went dark. The fog that had been dissipating reversed; black smoke replaced it, thick and directional, moving with the specific quality of something that had a source rather than a weather pattern. The green eyes formed in it, large, ancient, the same eyes from the dark room of the vision, the same eyes from the Reach tower recording, and resolved into the shape of a massive wolf standing in the smoke, looking at all of them. Delvrath. His voice was not a sound exactly; it occupied the air the way pressure occupies a sealed room. He told Styrr to fall back in line as he told the group that as long as Solveig was alive, their deal stood, and he mentioned that there were fates worse than death, and the rapid aging began. However, before he could, Styrr's hand came up as a beam of energy; the resistance made visible, had been building across thousands of years of small exercises of will, stopped the aging cold. Delvrath's attempt failed against it, and the ancient entity paused. This had not happened before; this was new. The binding was supposed to be total, and the person he had bound had apparently been quietly making it less total for a very long time without him fully registering the process. As he realized this, he looked at Chronus. He remembered their conversation and knew what he had said before had gotten through to him. With a look that could only be described as a plea, he simply exclaimed, “Brother, do not let me return to that darkness…” He said it with the precision of something that had had a very long time to understand exactly which words would reach exactly which wounds. Chronus hesitated, then he steeled himself and picked up his staff and took Solveig hostage, telling Styrr to fall back in line. The group did not fully understand what had just happened. The shock of it was visible on everyone who had spent the past several months watching Chronus try to make things right after everything that had been done while he was not in control of himself. 
The group's decision lasted approximately as long as it took Mia to reach for the bear head. She threw it at Delvrath, where it landed in front of him, and then she detonated it. In the specific distraction of the aftermath, Volg grabbed Styrr while Wylarin came up behind Chronus and knocked him out cleanly, which was the most decisive and least complicated solution available. Solveig ran to Styrr as she did; Styrr had used what he had built over thousands of years to push Delvrath back, a temporary banishment, not a permanent one, nothing that broke the deal. As he did, Delvrath's voice came through as his presence dissipated: the deal still stood. He would come back; this was not an escape, this was a delay. The smoke cleared as the green eyes were gone and the road to Old Orsinium was open. They talked to Styrr in the aftermath while the sky finished returning to normal. He reiterated Calintar's involvement and emphasized something the group needed to understand clearly: Vos was innocent. Vos-Greel the person had no part in any of it; the Argonian they had known was in there somewhere and had been in there since the Ghostweld Crystal, watching all of it happen through his own eyes without being able to stop it. Whatever they did about Calintar, they needed to know that. As he finished explaining, Liliana slapped him; he said it was probably deserved. However, using the voice of someone completely serious, she said it was his job to make things right; he said he intended to. He then told them to finish the competition, as he would meet them at Clan Agalor. Chronus regained consciousness, assessed the situation, and teleported away in a bright flash without saying anything to anyone. The judges tallied the count in the specific bureaucratic calm of people who had heard some unusual sounds from down the road during the competition but had been instructed to focus on their work. Yegoth stood at the edge of the judging area watching Morning and Evening with the specific silence of someone who has lost and knows it and has not yet decided what to do about that. Then the announcement came: Clan Agalor, winner of the third competition and the series as a whole! Yegoth did not move; he did not react visibly beyond the silence that was already there. He stared at Morning and Evening with something that was past anger, the cold, specific thing that anger becomes when it has been running for a very long time and has finally found what it was always going to become. Behind him, for just a moment, a pair of green eyes appeared in the air and were gone before anyone could look directly at them. Back at Pariah's Pinnacle, the celebration was real in the specific way that celebrations are real when the thing being celebrated was genuinely uncertain for a long time. Liliana made food and drinks; people ate and drank and allowed themselves to believe for a few hours that the competition had resolved something. The mood was the kind that does not come often and does not stay long but is worth having while it is there. Suddenly Chronus came back. He came back with information rather than an apology, which was either avoidance or practicality or both, and there was no immediate occasion to determine which. He had been somewhere in the interval between teleporting away and reappearing, and what he had found out was this: Yegoth had made a deal with Delvrath. The Grey Snow forces were actively preparing an attack on Pariah's Pinnacle, and it was coming in the next few days. The celebration continued around this information because there was nothing useful to do with it immediately, and everyone in the room had been through enough to know that you took the good moments when they came. But the information was there, and it was real, and the celebration had a different quality after it than it had before. The competition was won, the deal between Yegoth and Delvrath was made, and the Grey Snow was coming.
