The Bloody Knoll sat just outside Clan Shatul with the particular atmosphere of a place that has hosted enough violence to be comfortable with it. The crowd that had formed inside was not the grim assembly the name suggested; there was food, there were drinks, there was the specific energy of people who had come to watch something happen and were enjoying the anticipation of it. Morning led her group through the entrance and found them a corner near the door where they could talk without being immediately swallowed by the crowd. She thanked all of them, the way gratitude comes out when it is real, and the person saying it is not accustomed to needing it. She tried to move directly into logistics, who would fight, how this would go, when Chronus interrupted with a question that had the quality of something he had been carrying since before they arrived. Why go through with this at all? He laid it out without cruelty; if Clan Agalor was suffering this much, perhaps the kindest thing was to stop. To let it go before the losing became total, just let dreams be dreams. Mevanos and Qrowe found themselves in the same current. Mevanos was careful to say he would honor his agreement with the clan, but was honest that he thought Morning should hear what Chronus was saying. Bayard came at it from a different angle, quieter, asking why Hermaeus Mora was necessary in the first place. Clan Agalor could be rebuilt somewhere else. Somewhere, Yegoth and the Grey Snow were not already waiting. Mia pushed back; she understood the fight to keep land because she had fought the same fight for her own. She told Morning to keep going; Wylarin agreed, with the particular weight of someone who had watched a village she loved fail to hold on and knew the cost of rebuilding; they had both been in versions of this room before. Gulraam had been quiet through all of it in the specific way that suggested he was thinking rather than simply waiting. When he spoke, it was with an odd sobriety that sat differently on him than his usual register. He said that if a clan needed a god to wipe their collective ass, they did not deserve to exist and should be ashamed, as Orsimer. He delivered this with the directness of someone who has simplified a complicated truth down to its honest center. Morning took all of it in; she respected Chronus's position, the son of Hermaeus Mora, saying the clan's connection to Mora was not worth this weight, which was not a small thing. But she could not make herself let go of what the clan meant to her mother. Before she walked away to organize the fight, Gulraam offered one more thing: no one should fight your battles for you. If the clan was going to survive, it had to find the strength to do it itself. She walked away to talk to Yegoth's people about the terms. Vos-Greel materialized in the group's corner shortly after Morning left, warm and slightly apologetic about appearing at all, explaining that since his resources were also being disrupted, he had a stake in how this competition resolved and wanted to see it through in person. Mia registered his presence with the particular alertness of someone who has not yet identified what is wrong but knows something is, since every time he has been there, something has gone wrong. The group continued its deliberation in Morning's absence, with Vos observing, with the accuracy of someone who listens well, that whatever Morning claimed about being outcasts from Orc society, she and Evening and Shel were still Orcs, and Orcs were stubborn in ways that made abstract arguments about strategic retreat land poorly regardless of how sound the arguments were. Wylarin had gone quiet; the tension in her was visible to anyone paying attention, and the conversation was eating at her in a way she had not voiced and was not going to voice in this setting. Suddenly, the crowd got louder, and Yegoth walked into the arena. Behind him, having made her decision somewhere between Gulraam's last words and the sound of the crowd, walked Morning. She had decided to fight herself. Vos-Greel turned to Mevanos, Qrowe, and Wylarin with the casual timing of someone who has been waiting for exactly this moment. He mentioned that the Grey Snow was camped near Clan Shatul. Everyone was at the competition, so if there was information to be found about what they knew regarding the Ivory Wolf, the window was now. He suggested they go look, and they went, leaving Gulraam, Bayard, and Mia to watch the fight. Chronus stepped outside for fresh air. And Kushrakh, who had heard about the competition on his way in and had his own reasons for wanting to be present, walked through the entrance looking for Yegoth. Outside, the three moved through the Grey Snow camp with different methods and different results. Mevanos went invisible and encountered an Orc who had stayed behind, clutching his stomach with the particular misery of someone whose body had made an executive decision about his afternoon. Mevanos assisted the process along with a concoction that made the decision considerably more urgent, sending the Orc in the opposite direction at speed. Inside the tent, he found a partially burned document, most of it gone, one section recoverable. A route schedule through the Northern Pass to Clan Agalor. An asset was confirmed at the route on three separate occasions. A note warning Grey Snow patrols to keep their distance when the asset was active because its behavior in proximity to their own people had been unpredictable. Qrowe bent the shadows around himself and moved through the camp until a box chose that moment to fall over beside him for reasons that had nothing to do with him and everything to do with the seeming cruelty of inanimate objects. A dog found this interesting and approached with the investigative intent of an animal that had found something worth barking about. Sleeping powder resolved the situation before it escalated. In the tent, he found a second burned document, also partially recovered. A personal note in a voice that was blunter and more direct than the careful professional language of the route schedule. The relevant section: an insistence that the arrangement was useful but did not define the writer's purpose. That Clan Agalor was not holding their land was always going to happen. The arrangement simply made the timing cleaner. A man explaining to himself, in a document he expected no one else to read, that his reasons were his own. Wylarin moved through the camp without incident and found Yegoth's personal tent. Inside, fully intact, was a letter; she read it where she stood and then put some rocks in Yegoth's spare shoe before she left. The letter confirmed the arrangement, the asset's deployment, and the supply disruptions. It confirmed that whoever was writing it had no intention of letting the competitions determine the outcome; the competitions were public cover for something that would happen regardless of who won a fight, a race, or a hunt. All three left with what they had found. Inside the Bloody Knoll, the fight had begun. It was a steady exchange at first, Morning giving as much as she received, neither side immediately overwhelming the other. But the math of it was apparent to anyone watching carefully. Yegoth had stamina that came from decades of exactly this, and Morning was burning through hers faster than she was building toward anything decisive. He was also watching the crowd; specifically, he was watching Kushrakh, and after nearly every significant exchange, he would find Kushrakh's eyes in the crowd for just a moment. He knew he was there, and he was making sure Kushrakh knew that he knew. The combination worn on Morning, she took the hits and kept moving, but she was losing ground and losing time, and the crowd could feel it. Eventually, she was forced to the edge of the ring. Gulraam called out to her from the sidelines with the specific force of someone who has decided that this moment requires his full attention. It was enough; she got up, and she was immediately met with two punches to the face and went down. Yegoth walked back into the crowd with the composure of someone for whom this was always the expected result. The Fighters Guild members came out to patch Morning up and clear her from the ring. Afterwards, Yegoth found Kushrakh in the crowd; he was arrogant about it in a specific way that was worse than anger: dismissive, unconcerned, treating the history between them as something not worth the energy of acknowledgment. Kushrakh called this out directly and challenged him, which Yegoth accepted with the ease of someone who considers the answer obvious. They got back in the ring, and the crowd, which had been dispersing, reconvened with the energy of people who had been given an unexpected second act. Chronus came back inside during this fight; his robes smelled of ash in a way that raised questions. The second fight was different from the first. Kushrakh and Yegoth were more evenly matched, and both of them knew it. What Kushrakh had that Morning was pure fighting experience; he had been in those pits longer than Morning could have ever imagined. He won; however, it was not easy, and it was not clean, but it was decisive enough that the result was clear. He ignored the crowd that formed around him to look across the ring at Yegoth and tell him they were not done and would fight again. Then he joined the group clustered around Morning, who was battered and awake and beating herself up with an efficiency that exceeded anything Yegoth had managed physically. Wylarin started on her wounds and her words simultaneously. Morning was not receiving either easily. She could not understand how she had let this happen again, let him beat her, in front of everyone, the same way it had always gone. In the specific desperation of that moment, combined with Chronus’s comments, she turned to Gulraam and Kushrakh, the two Orcs of the group, and asked them a very simple question: “Why do either of you care?” She listed the failures; the entire of Wrothgar is against them, and they are the furthest thing from what an Orc should be. Two Orcs who knew all of this: why would they stay when she was a disappointment to everyone who looked at her? Gulraam never really gave her a direct answer but told her it was not over; two competitions remained. He told her to pick herself up and keep going. Morning pushed everyone aside and walked out into the cold Wrothgar snow, still not certain what to do with any of it, just needing the cold and the distance from the crowd and the question that had not resolved itself yet.
