The days between the competition's end and the Grey Snow's arrival had a specific quality to them: the particular busyness of people who know something is coming and have decided that the most useful thing they can do with the time is prepare rather than wait. Clan Igrun's fighters arrived and got to work teaching the members of Clan Agalor. The younger Clan Agalor members were put through forms of combat that most of them had never considered before. The older members learned spear tactics designed for exactly this kind of situation: holding a narrow space against a larger force, keeping distance, making numbers count for less than they should. Granny Lexy arrived during this and said she had been sent by the Reach Witches because they considered Delvrath a Reachman issue and intended to have a hand in resolving it. She said this with the specific certainty of someone who has already decided what she is going to do and is informing rather than requesting. She took Mevanos and Ahriman aside, and they began formulating what to do about Delvrath specifically, away from the main preparation, in the particular quiet of people working on a problem that requires concentration. The defensive plan came together through a conversation between Clan Igrun's leadership and Morning: let the Grey Snow come, let them think they are pushing through, funnel them toward the underground section where the terrain belongs to the defenders. Bayard and multiple Clan Agalor members spent the following days building palisades and establishing the channels that would make the funneling work. It was unglamorous construction work done under time pressure with people who were not professional engineers but who knew this mountain better than anyone who would be coming to fight them. Evening was on the perimeter. He found them approaching from the direction of Clan Shatul territory, close neighbors, which was not a coincidence, and sent word. Mevanos, Wylarin, Ahriman, Qrowe, Liliana, Bjorn, and Volg came when called, with Bayard already present. Evening laid out the three-part structure with the efficiency of someone who had been carrying a plan and needed to hand it off cleanly. Team One consisted of Qrowe, Bjorn, Liliana, and Evening; they would hold the frontline, thin the Grey Snow's numbers, and funnel them toward the underground entrance. They were the visible edge of the whole plan, and they needed to be convincing about it. Team Two consisted of Wylarin, Bayard, Volg, and Morning, who would hold back and activate the magical explosive charges set throughout the cave once enough of the Grey Snow was inside. The instruction was simple and absolute: do not be in the room when they go off. Team Three would consist of Mevanos, Ahriman, and Granny Lexy, who would not fight. They would prepare; their target was not the Grey Snow but what was powering Yegoth. Evening said what needed to be said before everyone went to their positions. He said it without performance, just the plain truth of a man who had spent too many years being the quiet one and had decided that this was not the moment for quiet. They had been hiding for too long; this was their home. They had built it from nothing on a long-dead mountain, and they had filled it with people that the rest of Wrothgar's society had decided it did not need. That was exactly what they had always been told they could not have; he told them to show Wrothgar that they deserved to be there. Mevanos, Ahriman, and Granny Lexy teleported away to prepare their part. Everyone else went to their positions and waited while The Grey Snow introduced themselves with a battering ram. The crash at the front gate was the kind of sound that exists below the frequency of thought and lands directly in the chest. They came through into the hallway leading to the main chamber: numbers, momentum, the specific energy of a force that has been promised a quick resolution to a problem it has been working on for weeks. The door to the main chamber was not where they expected it. The Apocryphal Stone had sealed it, solid, wrong-looking, the specific texture of something that did not belong to the physical world but was very definitively present in it. The Grey Snow had nowhere to go but forward, and forward led toward the underground entrance. Evening and the younger Orcs took the initial contact. They held long enough to matter and gave ground carefully, not panicking, not breaking, retreating in the specific way that is different from fleeing because it has direction and purpose. They reached the entrance to the underground section, and the second layer of defense was already there. Qrowe opened with the dead; a summoned force of undead warriors occupied the narrow space, creating an obstacle that needed to be addressed before anything else could happen. The Grey Snow dealt with it because that was the only option available. Bjorn moved through the engagement with the particular efficiency of someone who had been in enough of these fights to stop thinking about it and start doing. Liliana had brought old cooking oil, something she had an abundance of, gathered from the kitchen with the specific practicality of a cook who understands that fire is a tool, and used it on the battlefield with the calm of someone who had decided that this problem required heat.
Yegoth finally reached the underground section; he looked different from the man at the Bloody Knoll, different from the man across the table in the Fighters Guild Hall. The armor had changed, the black and green hue of it, the Delvrath energy dripping from his form like something that had been poured into a container not quite the right shape for it. The eyes were angry and glowing. He was still Yegoth; the intelligence was still there, the specific authority of someone who has never needed to announce that they are in charge, but he was Yegoth amplified past what Yegoth alone could sustain. He found Evening and was not interested in anything else in the room. The fight between them was not equal, and both of them knew it. Evening was good; he had become better than anyone who knew him as the boy who stayed behind in Clan Shatul would have expected. He held the fight longer than he had any right to against what Yegoth had become. When the blow landed, the shield cleaved in half, the gash opening across his arm, but he did not fall. He retreated; he had always known the line between holding and retreating, and he crossed it correctly. As he passed Wylarin, he put the detonator in her hand and told her to use it when she felt the time was right. The cave filled with Grey Snow. Qrowe's Bone Colossus arrived in the space like a problem that needed all of the room's attention immediately. The signal to evacuate moved through the team. Wylarin watched the Colossus buy the time it needed to buy and pressed the detonator. Yegoth saw it coming. He saw the sequence, the Colossus, the evacuation, the specific quality of Wylarin's attention before she pressed the trigger, and he moved. He was crossing the distance between them when the cave came down. A massive section of rock chose him as its destination, and he caught it with both hands. He was holding up a section of mountain. The specific wrongness of Delvrath's energy made it possible for exactly long enough. He slammed the rock behind him and walked into the inner chamber where everyone was waiting with the specific posture of someone who had just carried a mountain and was ready to break through them next. He faced them; he was still standing. Everything they had done- the palisades, the funneling, the oil and the fire, the undead, the Bone Colossus, and the explosive charges had brought the fight to this room and had not finished it. He had come through all of it, and he was ready to take on whoever was left with the raw power of himself and whatever Delvrath had put in him. The inner chamber held its breath as Yegoth looked around the room with the specific attention of someone who is not interested in what is directly in front of them and is searching for what matters. Morning and Evening were not immediately visible; Wylarin, Liliana, and Bayard were between him and everything else, and he regarded them with the flat assessment of obstacles rather than opponents. He said he would make this place burn; he said no one would stand in his way, and he said it without the performance he had given in Orsinium, without the crowd he had been playing to at the Bloody Knoll. This was the private version of Yegoth's conviction, the one that existed when there was no one left to impress. Volg came swinging around the corner, committed and without announcement, where his sword found Yegoth's bare hands. Yegoth caught it as the blade cut into his palms, and he threw it aside without acknowledging the cuts, which established several things at once about the current state of Delvrath's influence and what it was doing to his pain response. His attention shifted to Volg, and through Yegoth's eyes, something else's attention shifted as well: "The Wolf versus The Fox, a tale as old as time." Volg grabbed him by the throat before the next move could happen, and the chamber reorganized itself around that point of contact. Wylarin's arrow should have ended the immediate problem; it was the right angle, the right timing, the right read of the moment. What arrived in her mind instead was something that had no business being there, Delvrath's influence pressing against her attention at exactly the wrong fraction of a second, redirecting by just enough. The arrow found his cheek instead of his eye. She called it out to the room immediately because everyone needed to know that the battlefield was not only physical. Liliana went for the legs; the armor that had grown around his knees was not armor in any conventional sense; it was Delvrath's energy made material, the kind of defensive response that does not come from training but from something older than training. Bayard's Aedric Spear went through his foot and into the ground beneath it; this worked. Yegoth was pinned for exactly the duration it took him to decide that the situation required a different response. The yell came from somewhere that was simultaneously Yegoth and not Yegoth; the register of Delvrath’s howl translated through a mortal body, which struck fear into the hearts of many. Everyone in the room was sent back; dark ink spread across the floor with the deliberate quality of something that knew where it was going, pooling and solidifying. Wylarin and Bayard were caught in it, but Volg and Liliana were not. After that, quills grew from his arm, green, large, belonging to nothing in the natural world, and crossed the room in a single motion. Volg took each of them with the specific stubbornness of someone who has decided that this is what is happening and began moving forward through it, but Yegoth sidestepped the charge. He was preparing the next response when the room changed; Chronus appeared, not physically, but as a visage. Everything stopped in the way that things stop when something unexpected arrives with sufficient authority. He spoke to Delvrath directly; he said he had access to a machine. He said the machine would give Delvrath what he had been working toward since before anyone in that room was born: recognition, power, the specific validation of being imbued with the energy of a forgotten Daedric Prince, named Ithelia. He named Moira as the machine's creator and Apotheosis as her goal; he said all Delvrath needed to do was leave Yegoth and come with him. Delvrath deliberated; the deliberation was visible in Yegoth's face in the specific way that two things occupying the same space create interference. A conflict between what Yegoth wanted and what Delvrath was considering; then Delvrath made his choice, and he left. Yegoth compressed back into the dimensions of a man who had been carrying something enormous and had suddenly been asked to be himself again. The quills dissipated, the ink retreated, and the pain that Delvrath's energy had been suppressing arrived all at once, and he cried out with it. The cry of a man whose arms had been reshaped by something that was no longer there to maintain the reshaping. Wylarin was the first to move; she kicked him directly in the area where the sun never showed. Liliana followed; she took hold of both of his disfigured arms, the arms that Delvrath's quills had grown through, the arms that had caught a section of mountain and thrown it behind him, and she pulled. Both arms separated from his body with the specific violence of someone who had decided that the price of the power he had sought was going to be paid in the most immediate currency available. She told him what this cost, and she told him clearly. However, he refused to surrender; he stood there armless and bleeding and screaming that he would burn this place to the ground with his children inside it as he spat at Liliana. Bayard looked at him with something that had pity in the neighborhood of it, not pity exactly, but the recognition of what pity would look like if it were warranted, and told him he could be healed. He told him he was not going to do it; with that, Yegoth said he would not have accepted it anyway and kept talking about Morning, Evening, and Shel in the specific way of someone who has committed so completely to a position that physical reality has stopped being relevant to it. Morning and Evening came through the door. Evening was still carrying his bloodied arm from their previous fight. They stood in front of the armless form of their father with the sadness of people looking at something they had been afraid of for most of their lives and finding that the fear and the sadness were the same thing from different angles. He tried to headbutt Morning, but Evening caught his head with his bloodied hand before it connected. He said quietly, "You will never hurt us anymore." And then he did the thing that needed to be done, as he snapped his neck cleanly and without drama, and with that their father was gone. The chamber was silent with the specific quality of silence that follows something irreversible. As Morning and Evening stood in front of the lifeless father, tears started to stream down their face; how could they feel sympathy for someone who treated them so horribly? They couldn't understand it, as those around them tried to help them understand. Ahriman's Tomeshell arrived with the footage of what had happened elsewhere while the inner chamber was deciding itself. The pocket realm assembled from Moira's previous architecture received Delvrath in his full form, the massive wolf, the green eyes, the presence that occupied more of the space than his physical dimensions accounted for. He moved through it with the nervousness of something that has wanted something for a very long time and is not entirely certain the wanting is going to be satisfied. He found Mevanos and Ahriman and questioned them in a way that showed that he didn't fully trust them but still needed them, not aggressively, but with the complete attention of an ancient entity. After talking for a bit, they satisfied him as Chronus explained the Black Book. He explained the machine, explained the process by which Ithelia's contained power would be transferred. Delvrath considered the dimensions of the machine against the dimensions of his current form, made himself smaller, and stepped inside. The moment the machine activated, he knew something was wrong, but the finding came too late. Mevanos and Ahriman had worked on something that drained rather than transferred. The machine designed to harvest Apocryphal energy from those who entered it was doing what it had been designed to do. Delvrath understood the trick in the moment before the draining became irreversible and cried out to Chronus that he had trusted him, that he did not want to return to the darkness, that this was not what was supposed to happen. He gave Chronus one last look of betrayal as a single tear fell down Chronus’s face. Black smoke proceeded to fill the room, and out of the machine stepped a small white fox. It had none of what Delvrath had been; the memories were gone, the ancient cunning, the binding's mechanism, the thousands of years of specific grievances and strategies, all of it had gone into the Black Book. What remained was the original essence beneath all of that: Hircine's hunting instinct, Lorkh's blood, the thing that had risen from the earth before it knew what it was or what it wanted. The fox looked at Ahriman's leg and brushed against it with the complete innocence of something that has no framework for what it has just lost. As they looked, they noticed that the Black Book was not on its pedestal; it had been somewhere else before anyone thought to look for it. Footsteps were heard coming up the stairs as Styrr came up with Granny Lexy behind him, which answered the question of how he had arrived in a pocket realm constructed from a dead woman's failed Apotheosis attempt; she had brought him. He had heard about the plan and had needed to see; he found the fox and breathed. The relief was visible in the specific way of someone who had been carrying a fear they had not fully acknowledged and was now putting it down. Ahriman stopped him before he reached it and asked what he was doing. Styrr said what he felt without dressing it: sympathy, a fresh start, the possibility that something treated differently than he had been treated might become something different. However, Mevanos identified the remaining conflict: Hircine's essence and Lorkh's still within the fox, pulling in different directions, the specific unresolved tension of two forces that had never been naturally compatible. For the fox to be at peace, one of them had to be returned. Styrr was concerned about the deal; the deal that kept him and Solveig alive ran through Delvrath's existence. If Delvrath was diminished further, the deal's terms became uncertain; however, Chronus told him Delvrath would retain enough to hold the deal, not much more than that, but enough. Styrr agreed reluctantly in the way that people agree to things they have decided are right, but that cost them something. Mevanos held the fox and called upon Hircine to reclaim what was his. The power left visibly; the fox was smaller after, quieter, less present but still alive, still there. Mevanos handed the fox back to Styrr as Granny Lexy brought them both back to Tamriel with the efficiency of someone for whom this kind of transit is simply a tool rather than an event. As Styrr looked down at the fox in his arms, he tried to imagine what his life would be like after this point, finding nothing.
